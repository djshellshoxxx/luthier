## 1. Summary

The guitarrón mexicano is the bass of the mariachi ensemble: a very large, deep-bodied, **fretless**
six-string acoustic bass. Its back is strongly convex/V-arched, built in staves ("ribs") rather than as
a flat plate. It is tuned re-entrantly (A1 D2 G2 C3 E3 **A2**), so string 1 sits an octave above string 6.
Players usually pluck **two strings at once in octaves** with thumb and finger(s) to get enough
loudness to anchor an unamplified mariachi. Sources say it descends from the 16th-century Spanish
*bajo de uña*, a "fingernail-plucked" bass (snippet [1]). Tops are traditionally **tacote**
(*Gyrocarpus* spp.), and back and sides are Mexican **cedro** (*Cedrela odorata*). The
**guitarrón chileno** is a different instrument; see §1a.

### 1a. Disambiguation: guitarrón chileno

The guitarrón chileno is a 25-string plucked instrument from Chile. Its strings are grouped in 5
courses, plus two 2-string groups of high strings called *tiples* or *diablitos*. It accompanies
*Canto a lo Poeta* and is organologically related to the Renaissance/Baroque vihuela, not to the
mariachi bass (snippet [2]). Its data is not relevant here. There is also a *guitarrón argentino*
(a different instrument). The Mexican **vihuela** is described as "built along the same lines as the
guitarrón" (small, deep, V-back) (snippet [11]).

## 3. Tuning (A4 = 440 Hz, 12-TET)

Standard tuning (string 6 → 1). This is confirmed by several independent snippets [1][4][5].
**The brief's A1 D2 G2 C3 E3 A2 is correct.**

| String | Note | Hz | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | A1 | 55.00 | wound metal |
| 5 | D2 | 73.42 | wound metal |
| 4 | G2 | 98.00 | wound metal |
| 3 | C3 | 130.81 | nylon (wound or plain) |
| 2 | E3 | 164.81 | nylon |
| 1 | **A2** (re-entrant) | 110.00 | nylon. Exactly one octave above string 6 |

- Intervals from string 6 to string 2 are P4-P4-P4-M3, i.e. a guitar's A–E pattern a fifth below the
  guitar ("tuned a fifth below the guitar", snippet [5]). String 1 then drops back to A2.
- Fingered range: a fretless neck to roughly the body joint gives about an octave per string, so the
  highest notes are around E4–A4 (~330–440 Hz). This is INFERRED; no source gives the neck-to-body
  fret position.
- Alternate tunings: none documented in the sources found. A Google Groups thread "Guitarron tuning"
  exists [15] but was not read. Historical 5-string or gut-strung variants are mentioned only in
  passing (gut strings: snippet [3]).

## 4. Strings

Construction: three nylon trebles (nylon-wound on a nylon core, or thick monofilament) and three
metal-wound basses on a steel core (steel, bronze, brass or phosphor-bronze wrap) [16 snippet].
Traditionally the strings were gut, or gut wound with metal (snippet [3]). Sets are loop-end.

| Set | A2 (1) | E3 (2) | C3 (3) | G2 (4) | D2 (5) | A1 (6) | Materials | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| La Bella **GUI** | .107 NW | .080 NW | .098 NW | .067 W | .079 W | .095 W | nylon-wound nylon trebles; brass-wound steel basses | snippet [14] |
| D'Addario **MG10N** (normal tension) | .110 | .082 | .098 | .060 | .075 | .098 | clear-nylon-wound trebles; phosphor-bronze-wound, "NY Steel" core basses | snippet [17] |

- The string-to-note mapping is INFERRED from pitch order. La Bella lists its gauges "right to left
  facing the instrument"; the order used here gives gauges that rise monotonically as pitch falls
  within each material group, and both brands agree.
- In mm, La Bella: 2.72 / 2.03 / 2.49 / 1.70 / 2.01 / 2.41.
- **Tensions:** no manufacturer tension chart was found.
- **INFERRED** tension for the nylon trebles, treating them as solid nylon (ρ = 1140 kg/m³),
  T = (2 L f)² · ρπd²/4:

| String | L = 0.62 m | L = 0.66 m |
|---|---|---|
| A2 .107 | 123 N | 140 N |
| E3 .080 | 154 N | 175 N |
| C3 .098 | 146 N | 166 N |

These are rough upper-ish bounds. Nylon-wound construction and the unknown true density both shift
them by about ±30 %. They are still roughly 2× classical-guitar treble tension (~60–75 N), which fits
the instrument being described as "heavy gauge". No inference was attempted for the wound basses
(core diameter unknown).

## 5. Body / structural resonances

**No measured modal or air-resonance data for the guitarrón mexicano was found.** Searches in English
and Spanish (tesis, acústica, frecuencias de resonancia, UNAM/IPN theses, JASA) turned up only
classical-guitar work.

| Mode | Freq (Hz) | Q | Status | Derivation / source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A0 air (Helmholtz, coupled) | **~72 Hz (range 58–92)** | ~10–20 (assume, guitar-like) | INFERRED | f_H = (c/2π)·√(A/(V·L_eff)), with c = 343 m/s, L_eff = t + 1.7r, t = 3 mm. | T(1,1)1 top "breathing" mode (coupled) | ~140–190 Hz | ~20–40 | INFERRED | In guitars the first top mode sits ~1.5–2 × A0, and the two coupled (0,0) body modes lie ~100 Hz above Helmholtz [19 snippet]. The very light tacote top (SG ~0.24) and large area push it low |
| T(1,1)2 / back mode | ~200–260 Hz | ~20–40 | INFERRED | same heuristic. A V-back behaves differently from a flat braced back (stiffer along the keel, stave construction), so this is very uncertain |
| Higher top modes (T(2,1), T(1,2)) | ~250–450 Hz | ~30–50 | INFERRED | classical ratios scaled by ~0.7 |
| Neck/bending modes | NOT ESTIMATED | — | — | no data. A short, massive fretless neck is probably above the string fundamentals |

Modelling suggestion: a body bank of the 4 inferred modes above, with A0 near D2 and
T(1,1) near D3–F3. This gives strong radiation of the A1–D2 fundamentals mainly through the air mode.
It fits the anecdotal "powerful and resonant bass" (snippet [3]), but it is an assumption.

## 6. Excitation / playing technique

- **Octave pinch.**
  - The right hand plucks or "pulls" two strings at once, near the soundhole (snippet [4][11]).
  - Index, middle finger and thumb are all used; the right hand must be strong (snippet [4]).
  - Notes are usually doubled at the octave, and the re-entrant tuning is designed for this [1][4].
  - Strings 6 (A1) and 1 (A2) form an exact octave at the same stop position.
  - Other octaves use fixed finger offsets (arithmetic check): D2 on string 5 with D3 = C3 + 2 st on
    string 3; G2 on string 4 with G3 = E3 + 3 st on string 2.
- **Left hand.** Heavy strings on a fretless neck need strong stopping pressure. It is common to
  stop notes with the thumb of the fretting hand (snippet [4]). Fretless stopping gives glissando and
  free intonation (snippet [3]).
- **Role.** The guitarrón is the principal rhythm and bass instrument. Octaves on beat 1 are joined by
  walking lines to the next chord root (snippet [4]).
- **Apagón.** This means "stopping of strings with the hand, striking/stopping/muting the strings with
  the palm" (snippet [20], from a Mexican-music study guide). In mariachi rhythms (e.g. son jalisciense)
  it gives short, percussive, damped bass notes. No measured damping or attack data was found.
- **Attack (INFERRED).** Pluck with flesh and nail; the pull-type pluck on thick nylon or wound strings
  gives large displacement. Pluck position near the soundhole is ≈ 0.25–0.35 L from the bridge, which
  suppresses the harmonics near n ≈ 3–4. Two strings a period ratio of 2:1 apart are excited
  near-simultaneously.

## 7. Distinctive spectral traits

**No measured spectral centroid, inharmonicity or T60 data was found for the guitarrón.** The traits
below are INFERRED and need verification from a recording.

- **Octave doubling.** Doubling reinforces the even harmonics of the low note: the upper string's
  harmonics coincide with harmonics 2, 4, 6… of the lower one. The result is a strong perceived
  fundamental and a "hollow-free", thick sound, with small detuning/beating between the two strings
  because intonation is fretless.
- **Fundamental region.** Fundamentals are 55–~330 Hz. An inferred A0 near 70 Hz and top modes at
  140–260 Hz would give a formant-like boost in the 60–250 Hz band. A lightweight tacote top implies
  high radiation efficiency but also fast decay.
- **Inharmonicity.** Nylon trebles have a low Young's modulus, so B is very small (≲1e-5). The wound
  steel-core basses give B ≈ π³ E d_core⁴ / (64 T L²). This could not be evaluated because core
  diameter and tension are unknown; expect around 1e-5–1e-4 by analogy with acoustic bass strings.
- **Sustain.** Expect a short to moderate T60, perhaps 1–3 s on the open A1. Reasons: heavy nylon, the
  string losses of a fretless neck (finger-stopped, lossy termination), and the frequent apagón. The
  instrument is valued for punch and projection more than sustain.

## 9. DATA GAPS / paywalled

- **Scale length is unconfirmed.** The sources disagree: ~66 cm [5] vs 650–750 mm [1]. No maker
  spec sheet (Paracho Elite, Sevillano, Candelas, Delgado) listing scale was found.
  → *Owner measurement:* nut-to-saddle distance on 2–3 real instruments.
- **Nut width, soundhole diameter, body outline (upper/lower bout, waist, body length), side depth
  vs keel depth, and top thickness were not found.**
  → *Owner measurement:* tape measurements; a caliper on top thickness at the soundhole; body
  volume by rice/bead fill or by the formula from measured outline × mean depth.
- **Air resonance (A0) is not measured.** The ~72 Hz figure is purely inferred.
  → *Owner measurement:* sing or sweep into the soundhole, or tap the bridge with strings damped,
  and record 5 s. The FFT peak below 120 Hz is A0; its −3 dB width gives Q. Also check A0 with the
  soundhole blocked (it should vanish), which separates the air mode from the top mode.
- **Top/back plate modes are not measured.**
  → *Owner measurement:* tap-test impulse responses with strings damped. Use a small hammer or
  fingertip at the bridge and at the V-back keel, with a mic 30 cm in front and optionally a contact
  pickup. This yields frequencies, Q and relative gains for the mode bank. Chladni/glitter patterns
  on the top would identify the mode shapes.
- **String tensions are not published.**
  → *Owner measurement:* ask La Bella / D'Addario; or measure unit mass of each string (weigh a
  known length) and compute T = μ(2Lf)².
- **Inharmonicity, T60, attack and spectral centroid are not measured.**
  → *Owner measurement:*
  - Record single open strings (A1, D2, G2, A2) plucked mezzo-forte and fully damped afterwards.
    Extract partial frequencies (B fit), per-partial decay (T60 vs frequency) and centroid.
  - Record octave pinches (A1+A2) and apagón strokes to model two-string beating and the damping
    envelope.
- **Academic acoustics of Mexican instruments.** No guitarrón paper was found. Search leads that
  were not accessible:
  - UNAM acoustics & vibrations lab (Boullosa et al.) classical-guitar work; the ResearchGate paper
    "El Puente, Modos de Vibración y Radiación Sonora en Frecuencias Medias de la Guitarra Clásica".
  - IPN theses (tesis.ipn.mx) on guitar radiation patterns.
  - None of these cover the guitarrón specifically.
- **Wood data.** No E or damping values for tacote (*Gyrocarpus*). Only an SG of 0.24 for
  *G. americanus* was found (snippet).
- **Proxy blocking.** All primary pages were blocked by the egress proxy. Re-running with fetch
  access to wikipedia/organology/wmic.net (which has a museum instrument with likely measured
  dimensions) and the string makers' pages should firm up §2–4 quickly.
